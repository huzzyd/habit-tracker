## What each source says

### S2, the brainwash manual

1. Decide exactly what you want.
2. Write the goal in full detail.
3. Write it in the present tense on a card. Read the card through the day.
4. List every action that moves you toward the goal.
5. Order the list by priority.
6. Do one item from the list every day, without exception.
7. See the goal in first person, with all five senses.
8. Feel the goal as if it already happened.
9. Act as if the goal is only a matter of time.
10. Repeat steps 7 and 8 for 15 to 20 minutes on waking and before sleep.

The manual has no author and no citations. Its steps match known behavior-change techniques: goal setting, action planning, self-monitoring, mental imagery, and identity statements.

### S3, the magnetic frequency clip

Four steps, written on the whiteboard:

1. Meditate. Become still. Relax.
2. Regulate your nervous system. Slow down. Use breath work and somatic practices.
3. Release emotional blocks. Use journaling and voice notes. When you are triggered, ask why.
4. Respond instead of react. Affirm "everything is happening for my good." Practice radical acceptance.

### S5, the invincibility frequency video

Whiteboard content, in order:

- A circle of dots. The circle is the universe. The dots are possibilities. You occupy one area of it.
- A loop: "I see lack" to "I feel lack" and back. The speaker calls this the feedback loop to break.
- Three planes: S (spiritual, highest), M (mental: beliefs, emotions, focus, identity), P (physical, lowest). Labeled God, humans, matter.
- "The opponent": the ego self, a character created by the material world. It chases desire, status, and validation.
- Alignment above, logic and materialism below.
- Three steps. (1) Stop being created by reality. (2) Prioritize your state: open, calm, non-reactive, abundant, confident. (3) Do not assume the role of the divine. Act on intuition only.

Two passages matter for the product:

- 18:00 to 19:30. The video game metaphor. You are the player, not the character. The character was programmed by its environment. To reach the next level, change the character and unlock new traits.
- 05:30 to 06:30. The feedback loop. Break it by deciding the feeling first, then the belief, then what you see.

The framing throughout is vibration, quantum mechanics, Kabbalah, Christ and antichrist, and "reality obeys you."

## What the product leaves out

- All frequency, vibration, quantum, Kabbalah, and Christ framing. It contradicts the science positioning. It ends the university pilot. Daily cannabis users carry raised psychosis risk, so content that promotes magical thinking is contraindicated.
- "Do not assume the role of the divine. Act only on intuition." This is anti-planning. Implementation intentions, the strongest single mechanic in the concept doc, depend on planning.
- "Feel it as already achieved" as a standalone technique. See the evidence table.
- "Reality obeys you." No outcome claims of any kind. The product is a wellness product.
- "Brainwash" as in-product copy. Test it as a marketing hook only. It fails the pre-launch language review.
- "Ego desires: money, status." This conflicts with money reclaimed as the hero metric.

## Verified evidence

Verified against primary sources on 2026-09-02.

| Claim | Source | Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional Imagery Training (FIT) beats motivational interviewing for weight loss | Solbrig et al., Int J Obesity 43:883 (2019) | Confirmed | n=141. 6.44 kg vs 0.67 kg at 12 months. Two sessions only |
| Cravings are elaborated imagery. Competing imagery weakens them | Kavanagh, Andrade, May, Psychological Review 112:446 (2005). Retrospective: May et al. 2015 | Confirmed as theory | Elaborated Intrusion theory |
| FIT reduces substance use | Bakou et al. 2021 (student drinkers, 2-week and 4-week pilots). MIRAGE pilot (alcohol use disorder) | Pilot only | No cannabis trial exists. This is the pilot study candidate |
| Positive fantasy alone lowers effort | Kappes and Oettingen, J Exp Soc Psych 47:719 (2011) | Confirmed | Four experiments. Mechanism: premature relaxation |
| Implementation intentions raise goal attainment | Gollwitzer and Sheeran, Adv Exp Soc Psych 38:69 (2006) | Confirmed | d=0.65 across 94 tests, over 8,000 participants |
| Mindfulness-based relapse prevention beats standard relapse prevention | Bowen et al., JAMA Psychiatry 71:547 (2014) | Confirmed | n=286, single site. Fewer drug-use days and less heavy drinking at 12 months |
| Cyclic sighing improves mood and lowers arousal | Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine (2023) | Confirmed | 5 min per day for 28 days. No change in heart rate variability |
| Daily and high-potency cannabis use raises psychosis odds | Di Forti et al., Lancet Psychiatry (2019) | Confirmed | OR 3.2 daily, 4.8 high potency. Case-control. Causality contested |

## Open research questions

1. Does competing imagery at the craving moment reduce cannabis craving intensity? No trial exists. Candidate for the pre-registered pilot.
2. What reward schedule parameters keep the effect after rewards taper? Read the 2021 JAMA Psychiatry CM meta-analysis and Prendergast 2006 before you set defaults.
3. Does a night session in the pre-sleep cue slot reduce use on that night? Measurable in the pilot from craving logs.
4. Does the character frame reduce drop-off at onboarding compared with a plain profile? A/B test candidate.
